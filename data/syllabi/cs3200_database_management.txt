CS 3200: Database Management Systems
Instructor: Dr. Michael Torres
Term: Fall 2026

Overview:
Databases are the backbone of nearly every software system. This course gives you
a thorough grounding in both theory and practice. You'll learn to think about data
at scale — how to model it, store it, query it, and keep it consistent.

Course Goals:
The primary goal is to make you fluent in relational database design. You should
be able to look at a business problem and produce a normalized schema that handles
edge cases. We also want you to master SQL — not just SELECT statements, but
complex joins, subqueries, window functions, and performance optimization through
indexing and query plans. Transaction management and ACID properties are covered
extensively because real systems fail and you need to know what happens when they do.

We'll spend the last third of the semester on non-relational approaches. You'll
work with document stores (MongoDB) and understand when NoSQL is the right choice
versus when it's a hype-driven mistake.

By the end you should also be comfortable with database administration basics —
backup strategies, user permissions, and monitoring query performance in production.

Prerequisites: CS 2500, CS 3500

Evaluation:
  Homework (5 assignments)     30%
  Midterm Exam                 25%
  Final Exam                   25%
  Group Project                20%

The group project requires teams of 3-4 to design and implement a database-backed
application. You'll submit an ER diagram, normalized schema, sample queries, and
a brief written report analyzing your design tradeoffs.

Textbook: "Database System Concepts" by Silberschatz, Korth, Sudarshan (7th ed.)
Recommended: "Designing Data-Intensive Applications" by Martin Kleppmann
